The three-step recipe

Every prompt in this pack runs the same pipeline. Give the whole brief to Claude Code with the Higgsfield
MCP connected and it executes all three steps — or run the steps yourself in the Higgsfield UI.


1       Generate the product shot first (GPT Image 2).
        One clean e-commerce photo: product, simple surface, natural light, short label text — a brand name plus two short
        lines renders perfectly legible; a paragraph turns to gibberish. Set quality high. This image is your product's identity
        for every ad you ever make for it.


2       Generate the creator (Soul 2).
        An "unretouched iPhone front-camera portrait" in the exact room the ad happens in — bare skin texture, no beauty
        filter, amateur framing. The portrait's room, outfit and lighting should match the video prompt so the model locks
        onto one coherent scene. Keep the creator's job id: the same face can front an entire series.


3       Generate the ad (Seedance 2.5, one take).
        Pass both job ids as image_references with mode: omni_reference, 9:16, 1080p, duration 30, audio on. The
        prompt is a timestamped shot list: five beats, each with the exact line the creator speaks in quotes, plus a realism
        block and an audio spec. The model renders the performance, the voice and the lip-sync in one pass.


The five-beat script skeleton

Every 30-second ad in this pack follows the same arc, and the timestamps matter — they pace the
performance. 0–4s HOOK: mid-thought opener with the product already rising into frame. 4–11s WHAT
IT IS: name it, show the label. 11–18s DEMO: use it on camera — apply it, eat one, put it on. 18–25s
RESULT: the proof moment, leaning into the lens. 25–30s CLOSE: one low-pressure line, never a hard
sell. About 70–80 spoken words total fills 30 seconds at a natural pace — write more and the delivery
rushes.

Seven rules that keep it real

1       Product in frame within the first three seconds.
        Ads where the viewer instantly understands what's being sold convert measurably better. Script the product rising
        into frame during the hook line, never after it.


2       Write the realism block or get AI-slop.
        "Pore-level natural skin texture, flat daylight, authentic amateur framing, no beauty filter, no cinematic grade" —
        omit it and you get waxy skin, teal-orange bokeh, and an ad everyone scrolls past. Amateur is the aesthetic; don't
        let the model be cinematic.


3       Quote every spoken word, timestamped.
        Unquoted "she talks about the product" produces mumbling filler. Quoted lines at beats render clean lip-synced
        speech — and 70–80 words is the ceiling for 30 seconds.


4       Ban the sounds you don't want.
        End the audio spec with a short ban list — "no music, no water droplets, no bubbles, no synthesized blips." Native
        audio loves adding droplet tones; keep the list short inside a lean prompt or long prompts start failing.


5       Expect the mirror lottery.
        Some takes render mirrored like a real front camera — authentic, but your label reads backwards. It's consistent
        within a clip, so one ffmpeg hflip pass fixes every frame. Check the label before shipping, not after.


6       QA on frozen frames, not vibes.
        Pull a 3×3 contact sheet (ffmpeg tile filter) and check: exactly one product, two hands max, label legible, face
        matches the reference, no baked-in captions. Motion hides defects that stills expose instantly.


7       Volume is the strategy.
        Ad creative fatigues in about 72 hours. At roughly 390 credits per finished ad, generating a fresh variant twice a
        week costs less than one hour of a human creator — same recipe, new hook, new creator, same product job id.
